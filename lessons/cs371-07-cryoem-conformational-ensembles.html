Chapter 07: Cryo-EM Heterogeneity & Conformational Landscapes

Reading Time: 45 mins | Part III

The Resolution Revolution in Cryo-EM

Cryo-Electron Microscopy fires electrons at flash-frozen proteins, producing 2D projections. The computational challenge is reconstructing the 3D volume. When proteins exhibit continuous flexibility (conformational heterogeneity), tools like CryoSPARC and CryoDRGN use deep generative models to reconstruct a continuous landscape of states rather than a single rigid structure.

Deep Mental Model: Generative Volume Reconstructions

Instead of mapping all 2D images to one 3D consensus volume, we embed the images in a latent space and train an autoencoder or implicit neural representation to decode coordinates (x, y, z, and latent vector z) directly into electron density.

Algorithmic Implementation: Autoencoder for 3D Density

We use PyTorch to sketch an autoencoder that models volume deformations.

import torch
import torch.nn as nn

class VolumeDeformationAE(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, latent_dim=10):
        super().__init__()
        self.encoder = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Conv3d(1, 16, kernel_size=3, stride=2),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Flatten(),
            nn.Linear(16 * 15 * 15 * 15, latent_dim)
        )
        self.decoder = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(latent_dim, 16 * 15 * 15 * 15),
            nn.ReLU(),
            nn.Unflatten(1, (16, 15, 15, 15)),
            nn.ConvTranspose3d(16, 1, kernel_size=3, stride=2, output_padding=1),
            nn.Sigmoid()
        )

    def forward(self, x):
        z = self.encoder(x)
        return self.decoder(z)

model = VolumeDeformationAE()
# Dummy 3D volume (Batch, Channels, D, H, W)
dummy_volume = torch.rand(1, 1, 32, 32, 32)
reconstructed = model(dummy_volume)
print(f"Input shape: {dummy_volume.shape}")
print(f"Reconstructed shape: {reconstructed.shape}")
