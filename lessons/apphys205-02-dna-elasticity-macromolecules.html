Chapter 02: Polymer Physics & Macromolecular Elasticity

1. Ideal Chains and Persistence Length

DNA, RNA, and proteins are long chain-like molecules called polymers. The simplest physical model of a polymer is the Ideal Chain or Freely Jointed Chain (FJC), which views the molecule as a random walk of rigid segments of length $b$ that have no angular correlations. The mean squared end-to-end distance scales as $\langle R^2 \rangle = N b^2$.

Real polymers, like double-stranded DNA (dsDNA), possess bending stiffness. This introduces a length scale over which the polymer remembers its initial orientation: the persistence length ($\xi_p$). For dsDNA under physiological conditions, $\xi_p \approx 50 \text{ nm}$ (roughly 150 base pairs). This bending resistance forms the basis of the Worm-Like Chain (WLC) model.

2. The Worm-Like Chain and Marko-Siggia Equation

When you pull on a piece of dsDNA using optical tweezers, you are opposing entropy. As you stretch the molecule out to its contour length $L$, the number of available configurational microstates plummets, resulting in an entropic restoring force.

Marko and Siggia (1995) derived a brilliant interpolation formula for the force $F$ as a function of the extension $x$ for the WLC model:

$$ F(x) = \frac{k_B T}{\xi_p} \left[ \frac{1}{4(1-x/L)^2} - \frac{1}{4} + \frac{x}{L} \right] $$

Limiting Behaviors:
  • Small extensions ($x \ll L$): The force behaves linearly, akin to a Hookean spring: $F \approx \frac{3 k_B T}{2 \xi_p L} x$.
  • Large extensions ($x \to L$): The force diverges as $(1-x/L)^{-2}$, indicating that infinite force is theoretically required to perfectly straighten the polymer.

3. Simulating Optical Tweezer Data

Below is a Python snippet evaluating the Marko-Siggia WLC formula, producing a force-extension curve characteristic of single-molecule optical tweezer experiments.

import numpy as np

def marko_siggia_wlc(x, L, xi_p, T=298):
    """
    Computes the restoring force (in pN) for a WLC polymer.
    x: extension (nm)
    L: contour length (nm)
    xi_p: persistence length (nm)
    """
    k_B = 1.38e-23  # J/K
    k_BT_joules = k_B * T
    k_BT_pN_nm = k_BT_joules * 1e21  # 1 J = 1e21 pN*nm
    
    # Avoid singularity at x = L
    x_L = np.clip(x / L, 0, 0.99)
    
    term1 = 1 / (4 * (1 - x_L)**2)
    term2 = -0.25
    term3 = x_L
    
    F_pN = (k_BT_pN_nm / xi_p) * (term1 + term2 + term3)
    return F_pN

# Contour length of ~10 kb dsDNA
L_dna = 3400  # nm
extensions = np.linspace(0, 3350, 10)
forces = marko_siggia_wlc(extensions, L_dna, 50.0)

for x_val, f_val in zip(extensions, forces):
    print(f"Extension: {x_val:4.0f} nm | Force: {f_val:6.2f} pN")

Exercise: Freely Jointed Chain (FJC) comparison

The inverse Langevin function characterizes the FJC model. Write a script to plot both the WLC and FJC force-extension models on the same axis. Under what conditions do they deviate most?

Reveal Step-by-Step Solution

The WLC model heavily penalizes sharp bends, leading to a much steeper force divergence near the contour length compared to the FJC model. FJC assumes segments can rotate 180 degrees instantaneously.
